Pipeline

One message, from arrival to delivery

A message first lands in a durable queue, then deterministic gates and a reply rating decide whether to answer. If it does, Diana assembles context, generates with the model or the agent, and only after pre-send review sends the reply back to its source channel in chunks. Every stop leaves an outcome code and a reason.

01 Reply triggers

Deciding whether to answer

HandleEvent does not generate a reply directly. It writes the message into a SQLite inbound queue and wakes a worker, so a crash, a restart or a stuck model does not lose it: a worker claims an item with a 10-minute lease, and an expired lease is claimed again.

  1. HandleEvent

    The bot's own messages only go into history; pokes, joins, friend adds and recalls follow their own switches and cooldowns and never enter the queue.

  2. Private-chat admission

    Everyone / owner only / allowlist.

    Deniedignored_private_admission
  3. Register the direct call

    noteDirectedInbound runs before enqueueing and is later used to interrupt the same person's older reply.

  4. Write to the inbound queue

    Priority: direct trigger 100, quote reply 80, resolvable link 60, everything else 0; ties by time.

    Write failsretry journal replayed with 2–30 s backoff, plus a backfill
  5. Worker claims it

    By default 3 items per group and 2 per private chat in flight, under the global reply concurrency limit.

  6. A backfilled message?
    History onlybackfill_history_onlyOlder than the replay windowignored_stale
  7. Speech-to-text → route

    routeMessageEvent. A transient transcription failure retries the item later.

  8. Deterministic gates

    12 checks that do not call a model; see the table below.

    Stoppedignored_*
  9. Directly triggered?

    Private chat, a mention, a quote of the bot, an alias, an owner command, a resolvable link or a plugin command — after passing the reply gate (owner bypass → blocked → allowlist → exempt → active hours → member level).

    Nounprompted reply rating
  10. Is a reply to the same person generating?

    A message added within 2 minutes is classified by a model as a repeat, a supplement, a correction or an independent question.

    Supplement or correction above the thresholdthe old reply is regenerated merged_into_reply

Processing errors retry from 5 seconds, doubling up to 5 minutes, at most 5 times; after that the item is recorded as dropped_retries_exhausted. A definitive platform rejection is recorded as dropped_send_rejected and not retried.

01 Reply triggers

Backfill and gap detection

Messages sent while the bot was offline never arrive as pushes; they can only be pulled from the platform afterwards. runInboundCoordinator does that.

  1. Process start

    Releases expired leases and reads the last online checkpoint. While online the checkpoint is saved every 30 seconds.

  2. Heartbeat sees a disconnect or offline account

    Stops claiming and saves the checkpoint.

  3. Reconnected

    Replay cutoff = disconnect time − 30 minutes, looking back at most 24 hours.

  4. Three backfills

    After 1 second, then again at +1 and +5 minutes. Walks the group list and recent contacts, 100 per page and at most 200 per session, one session at a time.

  5. Deduplicate

    Compared with queued and stored messages, so nothing is processed twice.

  6. Should this one take part in replies?

    Only the newest N per session (history_backfill_message_limit, default 3) that would have triggered a reply directly are queued normally.

    The restfill in history only, no replies
  7. Queued normally
Why only the newest few

Coming back after an hour offline and answering a hundred stored group messages one by one would flood the chat. The recovered history still goes into context; it just does not trigger replies.

While online there is also gap detection: the first live message per group after a reconnect, and any jump in seq numbers, triggers verifyGroupSeqGap — a targeted refetch once backfill finishes, rechecked at 15 seconds, 1 minute and 3 minutes. It logs backfill_gap_detected / resolved / unresolved.

01 Reply triggers

Rating unprompted replies

Ordinary group messages that were not directly triggered first pass reply damping and the group's random sampling rate (owner exempt), then go to the router model. For each candidate it answers two things with a short reason each: relevance.directed (is this clearly addressed to the bot — yes or no) and chat_in.score (0–1, how natural it would be to chime in).

  1. Both switches off, or someone told the bot to stop in the last 10 minutes?
    Yessilent, no model call
  2. Build the rating input

    Addressing info, a time-ordered transcript and the group's extra criteria.

  3. Call the router model

    Purpose proactive_reply_router, bound to a chat model or the Jev decision model; waits at most 60 seconds.

    Timeout / errorsilentUnparsableask once more with a reminder, then silent
  4. directed = false and chat_in = 0?

    Means someone is asking it to stop, or a mechanical loop.

    Yessilent, even at "no limit"
  5. Answer-questions switch on and directed = true?
    Yespass replied_proactive
  6. Chat score meets the level, and the cooldown has passed?

    Cooldown defaults to 30 seconds and starts only after a chat reply is actually sent.

    Noignored
  7. Is the bot talking too much?

    Over 25% of messages in 10 minutes, at least 3, with more than one other person talking.

    Yes, below "no limit"silent
  8. Pass

    replied_proactive, into the reply flow.

01 Reply triggers

Concurrency, merging and stop

When one session gets messages quickly, three mechanisms avoid answering each one separately or answering the wrong thing:

Backlog hand-over

  1. Queued ≥ 30 seconds

    With newer messages behind it in the session; first attempt only, never the owner's.

  2. Held in a backlog

    At most 20 messages, kept 10 minutes.

  3. Taken by the next message

    The newest direct trigger becomes the reply target; without one, eligible candidates go to the rating together.

Merge while generating

  1. Same person adds a message within 2 minutes
  2. Repeat / supplement / correction / independent?

    Merged only at or above the confidence threshold (default 75%).

  3. Old draft discarded, regenerated

    Up to 3 times; no merging after chunks started sending or a side effect happened.

Interrupt

  1. The same person addresses the bot again in a group
  2. The old reply is dropped before sending

    superseded_follow_up; a recall only removes the quote, it never cancels a reply.

Group stop

Mentioning, quoting or naming the bot in a group with "shut up" or "stop" is caught by the pre-send review, which opens a 10-minute window for that group: the rating is skipped and in-flight unprompted replies are dropped. Mentions, quotes and names are still answered — "stop" means "stop butting in", not "ignore me".

02 Reply flow

After deciding to answer

replyAndRecord runs replyTo up to 3 times: when the same person adds a supplement or correction during generation, the old draft is discarded and regenerated with it; before the third attempt the turn is sealed against further merges.

  1. Prepare

    Current images, history, identity aliasing, request timeout, the typing indicator (Telegram and OneBot private chats).

  2. Owner or status command? Only a resolvable link?
    Commandsent directly, no reviewLinkresolved only, deduplicated by resource
  3. Plugins

    May start background tasks or answer directly.

    Direct plugin replyreviewed and sent without the chat model
  4. Assemble context and tools

    Prompt order below. Reply rules can pin this turn's model profile or turn the reply into voice.

  5. Agent on and allowed tools for this relationship?
    Yesagent runNoone model call; images or audio use the vision group
  6. Shape the reply

    Take out control markers; stop tool calls written as prose; convert to plain text where the platform has no rich text; split long replies at natural boundaries, then split code blocks, then compress with a model (30 seconds, at most 2 calls per reply).

  7. Pre-send review
    Stoppedthe matching ignored_*
  8. Send in chunks

    Next section.

    A supplement arrivedback to Prepare, regenerate
  9. Record

    History, message IDs, delivery stages, outcome; chat cooldown starts; relationship evaluation is queued.

Prompt order

System head (SOUL.md, fixed rules, tool rules) → summary checkpoint → stable history → cache breakpoint → per-turn blocks (plugin context, session state, memory, notebook, world book, self-notes, media index, recent sources, recent tool calls) → same-turn candidates → dependency images → system tail (speaker) → clock → decoration rules → current message. Everything before the breakpoint stays the same between turns in a group, so upstream prefix caches hit. Real user IDs and group numbers are replaced by aliases in model requests and restored in replies and tool calls.

When generation comes back empty, markers decide the fallback: a pause sends one in-character line and goes quiet; a refusal sends fixed text; a queued image job turns "started generating" into the reply; if say already spoke this turn it stays silent; otherwise "I didn't produce a usable reply."

02 Reply flow

Sending in chunks

  1. Split

    On [diana-msg] / [diana-line], trailing full stops removed; replies in the same session queue instead of interleaving.

  2. OneBot and over the forward threshold?
    Yessend a merged-forward card (its text gets its own account-safety check); on failure before the timeout, fall back to chunks
  3. Chunk by chunk

    Only the first chunk quotes and mentions; no quote if the trigger was recalled or the reply targets another participant.

  4. Pacing

    From the second chunk: max(300 ms interval, 100 ms per character), capped at 6 seconds, with the typing indicator kept on.

  5. Single exit: sendOutgoingWithResult

    Mask secrets → prepare local images and audio → resolve [diana-reply] / [diana-at] → send-time guards.

  6. Already sent this step?

    Fingerprints are kept in the turn's delivery ledger.

    Yesskip, reuse the message ID
  7. Lease the media share link

    Renewed to at least ten minutes ahead before every retry.

  8. executeOutboundCall
    Kicked / mutedgive upConnection offlinefail fast; the queue retries the whole turn laterGroup send failsseparate text and media lanes back off exponentially, then a drop cooldown
  9. Success

    Record Acknowledged, write chat history, keep the message ID, pause the typing indicator.

A chat reply is attempted once per call; platform media-validation rejections retry briefly, at most 3 attempts in all. send_retry_attempts is for non-reply sends such as scheduled pushes. When the queue retries a whole turn, chunks already delivered are skipped via the ledger and never sent twice.

03 Agent run

One thing per step

With the agent on and tools allowed for this relationship, Runner.Run produces the turn: every step the model either calls one tool or finishes with agent_finalize.

  1. Configure

    12 steps by default (max 16); the owner gets 16 steps and 20,000 characters of final reply, everyone else 8,000. 60 seconds per tool, 20 seconds reserved for finishing, 3 protocol repairs.

  2. Assemble tools

    Core tools are always callable; the rest appear as one catalog line and need tools_load, then tools_execute. Trimmed by the permission allowlist and bot / group extension switches; a tool without permission answers "no permission" instead of "does not exist". In groups, loaded tools are restored from the group session snapshot on later turns.

  3. Runner.Run starts

    Protocol prompt and deferred-tool catalog; Skills injected by trigger words; a trusted clock; the carry-over archive if the last turn was forced to finish.

  4. Steps left, and enough time to finish?
    Noforced finish
  5. Call the model

    Planning deadline = request deadline − finish reserve (the configured value or a third of the remaining time, whichever is smaller).

    Planning deadline hitforced finishOther errorhandled by the reply flowPlain text / agent_finalizefinish checks
  6. Run exactly one tool call

    Other calls in the batch get "not executed", so every step sees the previous result.

  7. Pre-execution checks

    Deferred tools must be loaded and pass schema checks → unknown or denied → input coercion → extension changes need a confirmation code → identical to the previous call is skipped → quotas (web_search 3; introspection 6 and tools_load 4 without using steps).

    Failsone protocol repair, back to the step check; forced finish when repairs run out
  8. Run the tool

    Per-tool timeout and output budget; run_command passes the command allowlist and runs in a sandbox as configured; MCP has its own 60-second timeout.

  9. Shape the result

    Secrets masked, long output truncated; search and render results go into the evidence ledger; remaining budget attached. Tool errors are not fatal — they become observations for the model.

    Next stepback to the step check

Finish checks

  1. silent = true?
    Yes, no image job queuedno reply this turnImage job queuedsilence refused, one repair
  2. Real line breaks in the text?

    Chunks must use [diana-msg] / [diana-line].

    Yesone repair
  3. Evidence required but no search? A link never fetched this run nor seen in the conversation?
    Yesone repair
  4. Internal protocol words leaked? Empty text?
    Yesone repair
  5. Return the reply

A forced finish happens when steps or repairs run out, the finish reserve is reached, or evidence was required and not gathered: a finish instruction and the evidence digest are appended, and only agent_finalize is offered, forced. Afterwards a forced finish keeps up to 12 step summaries as a carry-over archive (valid 10 minutes); the last 8 tool calls are kept for 30 minutes and injected into later turns.

Interim say

At most 3 per turn, 80 characters each, no line breaks; sent immediately and the typing indicator comes back. After it, the turn is no longer merged and regenerated, and it skips pre-send review.

subtask

A synchronous model call with no tools and no history: at most 4 per reply, 6,000 characters of material, 90-second timeout.

Context over budget

Summarise the longest text messages (at most 2 calls), replace images with descriptions, downsize images, and finally drop them.

Model errors

The runner does not retry. The provider stack retries a transient error once after 700 ms, halves an oversized context up to 4 times, then fails over to the next profile in order, keeping completed tool steps.

04 Browsers

Disposable headless rendering

  1. browser_render or the web-render plugin

    The plugin renders at most 2 links per message and injects the text as an untrusted block.

  2. Public address check

    http/https only; private, loopback, link-local, localhost and .local addresses are refused.

    FailsrefusedGitHub /releases linkthe official API first
  3. Take a slot

    3 at once by default, queueing at most 20 seconds; queue time does not count against the timeout.

    No slottoo many disposable browsers running
  4. Start Chrome

    A temporary 0700 profile, HOME / XDG redirected into it, local host names blocked in resolution.

  5. Navigate

    Every main-document request is re-validated; a download aborts the render.

  6. Wait until the page settles

    DOM, network and timers quiet; 25 seconds by default, 60 at most.

    Timeoutuse the last non-empty snapshot; error only if there is nothing
  7. Return URL, title, text

    The process ends and the temporary directory is deleted.

  • Only main-document requests are intercepted and re-checked; sub-resources such as XHR and images are not validated one by one.
  • HTML to image: simple pages use Chrome --screenshot (8 MiB output cap, takes a slot); scripted or animated pages use html_capture on a fake diana-render.invalid origin where every request is answered locally or blocked, with a strict CSP and a virtual clock, at most 600 frames and 4096 px — it does not take a slot today.
  • Finding Chrome: configured path → DIANA_HEADLESS_BROWSER_EXECUTABLE → DIANA_AGENT_BROWSER_EXECUTABLE → common install locations (including a downloaded Chrome for Testing) → PATH. Installation tries the system package manager first; on glibc Linux it falls back to downloading Chrome for Testing into DIANA_BROWSER_DIR.

05 Pre-send review

The audit model

The audit is simply on or off (on by default, overridable per group). One call judges all of the following; if none applies it is skipped entirely. Verdicts apply in this order:

  1. Audit call failed?
    Unprompted replysilent: when unsure, don't butt inDirect replysent: a bot that goes mute whenever a model is down is worse
  2. Private closing / stop

    Judged only within 3 minutes of the bot's last private message; only verdicts at 0.90 confidence or higher count.

    Stopignored_stop_requested; non-owners paused 30 minutesGoodbyes past the grace countignored_conversation_closed
  3. Group stop

    Judged on group messages that mention, quote or name the bot; opens a 10-minute window, and this reply is still sent.

  4. Loops

    Bot-to-bot loop detection is on by default. No content or no purpose lowers the reply appetite.

    Repeating itselfignored_self_repeat3 hits in 30 minutes, not the ownerpause with a notice ignored_ai_reply_loop
  5. Account safety score < 0.10?

    Deliberately low: suspected risk still passes; only a near-certain unsafe verdict stops it.

    Yesa neutral public notice instead (can be rewritten in persona)
  6. Quality (unprompted replies only)

    Contradiction, off topic, truncated, harmful advice, or send_confidence below proactive_reply_threshold.

    Failsignored_proactive_reply_quality
  7. Pass

    A refusal (confidence ≥ 0.90) is still sent; 4 refusals within 30 minutes start a pause.

The order is deliberate: once someone says "stop replying", whether this reply is accurate no longer matters, so closing comes first. The quality gate only applies to unprompted replies — going silent on someone who addressed you because of a score looks like ignoring them. Direct plugin replies go through the same audit but not semantic dedup.
